# Case note — Corticeira Amorim, FY2025

*One page to read before an interview. Figures from the engine on the published FY2025 consolidated accounts (2025 annual report, ESEF, read from the printed statements and note 19); every one is in the stored memo and passes the checker.*

## Context

Corticeira Amorim is the world's largest cork company: stoppers for wine and spirits, plus floor and insulation materials. It is a family-controlled group listed on Euronext Lisbon. Cork has to age before use, so the business carries a lot of stock.

## Three talking points

**1. Strong cash in a weaker year: where did it come from?**
- *The number:* revenue fell 8.3% to €860,967k, but operating cash flow was €219,015k, 155.4% of EBITDA. Inventories fell from €466,545k to €388,297k.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* releasing working capital produces cash once. A buyer normalises it out of free cash flow and checks that the peg is not set after the release.
- *Question for management:* "Which working capital items explain operating cash above EBITDA in FY25?"

**2. Working capital is half of revenue.**
- *The number:* net working capital is €439,589k in FY25, 51.1% of revenue. Inventories are 45.1% of revenue, against 16.3% for listed packaging peers.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* with this much working capital, the peg is one of the largest price items. It needs 12 to 24 monthly balances, because cork is bought seasonally.
- *Question for management:* "What are the monthly working capital balances for the last 24 months?"

**3. Debt refinanced; cash shown net of overdrafts.**
- *The number:* debt due within 12 months fell from 56.3% in FY24 to 28.7% in FY25 (R11 fired on FY24). Cash in the cash flow statement is €29,451k below balance sheet cash, explained by bank overdrafts in note 19.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* the definition of cash in the SPA must say whether overdrafts are debt, or the same €29m is counted twice or not at all.
- *Question for management:* "Are the bank overdrafts of note 19 included in interest-bearing debt on the balance sheet?"

Also worth a sentence: capex was 0.72x and 0.71x D&A in the two years (R24), so maintenance needs are a question.

## Limits of the analysis

- Annual accounts only; only note 19 was read. Other financial liabilities (€43,264k current) are still to assess.
- The published EBITDA is €140,985k and the lines rebuild €140,983k: the €2k is the report's own rounding, inside the reading check tolerance.
- The 7 key documents of a data room (debt schedule, inventory ageing…) are not public (R18).
- A first pass for an analyst to review: no view on the shares or the price.
