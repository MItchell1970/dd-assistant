# Case note — Corticeira Amorim, FY2025

*One page to read before an interview. Figures from the engine on the published consolidated accounts FY23 to FY25 (2025 and 2024 annual reports) and the notes of the 2025 report, read from the printed text of the ESEF file; every one is in the stored memo and passes the checker.*

## Context

Corticeira Amorim is the world's largest cork company: stoppers for wine and spirits, plus floor and insulation materials. It is a family-controlled group listed on Euronext Lisbon. Cork has to age before use, so the business carries a lot of stock.

## Three talking points

**1. Strong cash in a weaker year: where did it come from?**
- *The number:* revenue fell 8.3% to €860,967k in FY25, but operating cash flow was €219,015k, 155.3% of EBITDA. Inventories fell from €466,545k to €388,297k. In FY23 the same ratio was 14.9% (R10).
- *Why it matters in a deal:* releasing working capital produces cash once. A buyer normalises it out of free cash flow and checks that the peg is not set after the release.
- *Question for management:* "Which working capital items explain operating cash above EBITDA in FY25?"

**2. Working capital is half of revenue.**
- *The number:* net working capital is €443,570k in FY25, 51.5% of revenue, and €71,514k below the average of the three year-ends.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* with this much working capital, the peg is one of the largest price items. It needs 12 to 24 monthly balances, because cork is bought seasonally.
- *Question for management:* "What are the monthly working capital balances for the last 24 months?"

**3. The notes change what counts as debt.**
- *The number:* debt due within 12 months fell from 56.3% in FY24 to 28.7% in FY25 (R11). Note 22 shows the 2024 figure was commercial paper of €55,150k and a bond of €30,000k falling due. Note 24 shows that "other financial liabilities" of €43,264k are mostly sundry creditors, taxes and VAT.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* a line called "financial" is not always debt. Reading the note moves most of it from net debt to working capital, which changes both the price deduction and the peg.
- *Question for management:* "Which items within other financial liabilities are debt-like?"

Also worth a sentence: cash in the cash flow statement is €29,451k below balance sheet cash, explained by bank overdrafts in note 19; and of other income of €17,421k, note 30 leaves €7,051k as an unexplained "Outros" (R04, partly explained).

## What the notes settled

- **Short-term debt (note 22):** explained: commercial paper and a bond, since refinanced.
- **Other financial liabilities and assets (notes 24 and 14):** explained: mostly operating and tax balances.
- **Other income (note 30):** partly explained: the largest line is a residual.
- **Cash conversion and capex below depreciation:** still open.

## Limits of the analysis

- Annual accounts only: no monthly balances, so the working capital peg is indicative.
- The published EBITDA is €140,985k and the lines rebuild €140,983k: the €2k is the report's own rounding, inside the reading check tolerance.
- The 7 key documents of a data room (debt schedule, inventory ageing…) are not public (R18).
- A first pass for an analyst to review: no view on the shares or the price.
