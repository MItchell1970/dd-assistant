# Case note — TAP Air Portugal, FY2025

*One page to read before an interview. Figures from the engine on the published FY2025 consolidated accounts (2025 Integrated Report, pp. 152–156, notes 10 and 20); every one is in the stored memo and passes the checker.*

## Context

TAP is the Portuguese flag carrier, owned by the State after the 2020 rescue. In 2026 it is a live M&A process: Air France-KLM and Lufthansa submitted binding offers on 29 July, and the Government extended the deadline to complete the privatisation to 31 December 2026 (Jornal de Notícias, 6 September 2026, ["Governo prorroga até final do ano prazo para concluir privatização da TAP"](https://www.jn.pt/economia/artigo/governo-prorroga-ate-final-do-ano-prazo-para-concluir-privatizacao-da-tap/18123884)). This context is not used by the engine or the memo, which rely on the accounts only.

## Three talking points

**1. Net debt is a definition, not a number.**
- *The number:* borrowings less cash are -€43m in FY25: cash exceeds bank borrowings. Adjusted net debt is €2,293m, 3.16x EBITDA, once aircraft leases (IFRS 16) and pensions are counted. The 3.16x belongs to the €2,293m; on -€43m there is no meaningful ratio.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* enterprise value to equity deducts net debt. Which leases, pensions and provisions count is negotiated in the SPA, and a few hundred million move between buyer and seller.
- *Question for management:* "Which leases have purchase options, and what are the return conditions and maintenance obligations?"

**2. EBITDA under IFRS 16 is not comparable with pre-IFRS 16 multiples.**
- *The number:* reported EBITDA is €726m in FY25. Less the lease payments of €464m, it is €262m.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* an EV/EBITDA multiple must use EBITDA and net debt on the same basis. Leases are either in both or in neither.
- *Question for management:* "How is the fleet financed (leases, renting), and is it all in reported debt?"

**3. Working capital is negative, and that is the airline model.**
- *The number:* net working capital is -€598m in FY25. Liabilities from unused flight documents, tickets sold but not yet flown, are €711m.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* passengers fund the business before they fly. A peg set on a seasonal low, or a closing in the summer peak, moves the price; ticket liabilities could also be argued as debt-like.
- *Question for management:* "How seasonal is the balance of unused flight documents, month by month?"

Also worth a sentence: the effective tax rate was 96.9% in FY25 (R25), so net profit was only €2.1m.

## Limits of the analysis

- Annual accounts only: no monthly balances, so the working capital peg is indicative.
- Read from the English translation of the report; the Portuguese original prevails. Only notes 10 and 20 were read.
- The 7 key documents of a data room (debt schedule, facility agreements, AR ageing…) are not public (R18).
- A first pass for an analyst to review: no view on the offers, the price or the privatisation.
