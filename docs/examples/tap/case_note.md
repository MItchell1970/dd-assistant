# Case note — TAP Air Portugal, FY2025

*One page to read before an interview. Figures from the engine on the published consolidated accounts FY23 to FY25 (2025 Integrated Report, pp. 152–156, and the 2024 annual report) and ten notes of the 2025 report; every one is in the stored memo and passes the checker.*

## Context

TAP is the Portuguese flag carrier, owned by the State after the 2020 rescue. In 2026 it is a live M&A process: Air France-KLM and Lufthansa submitted binding offers on 29 July, and the Government extended the deadline to complete the privatisation to 31 December 2026 (Jornal de Notícias, 6 September 2026, ["Governo prorroga até final do ano prazo para concluir privatização da TAP"](https://www.jn.pt/economia/artigo/governo-prorroga-ate-final-do-ano-prazo-para-concluir-privatizacao-da-tap/18123884)). This context is not used by the engine or the memo, which rely on the accounts only.

## Three talking points

**1. Net debt is a definition, not a number.**
- *The number:* borrowings less cash are -€43m in FY25: cash exceeds bank borrowings. Adjusted net debt is €2,293m, 3.16x EBITDA, once aircraft leases (IFRS 16) and pensions are counted. The company's own net debt in note 19 is €817m, because it leaves out the leases without purchase option.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* enterprise value to equity deducts net debt. Which leases, pensions and provisions count is negotiated in the SPA, and a few hundred million move between buyer and seller.
- *Question for management:* "Which net debt definition do the facility agreements and the covenants use?"

**2. EBITDA under IFRS 16 is not comparable with pre-IFRS 16 multiples.**
- *The number:* reported EBITDA is €726m in FY25. Less the lease payments of €464m, it is €262m.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* an EV/EBITDA multiple must use EBITDA and net debt on the same basis. Leases are either in both or in neither.
- *Question for management:* "How is the fleet financed (leases, renting), and is it all in reported debt?"

**3. The notes turn "provisions" into a debt-like question.**
- *The number:* provisions are €415m at the end of 2025. Note 18 says €291m of it is aircraft redelivery costs and €78m is legal claims.
- *Why it matters in a deal:* redelivery costs are cash that leaves when a leased aircraft goes back. A buyer argues they are debt-like; a seller argues they are operating.
- *Question for management:* "What is the schedule of aircraft redeliveries and the cash cost by year?"

Also worth a sentence: EBITDA fell from €832m in FY23 to €726m in FY25; net working capital is -€598m in FY25, normal for an airline; and total assets of FY24 moved from €5,739m to €5,797m between the two reports (R30, a restatement).

## What the notes settled

- **Leases (note 19, p.207):** explained. The note gives the maturities; the lease liabilities tie to the balance sheet.
- **Provisions (note 18, pp.204–205):** explained, and split by nature.
- **Unused flight documents, "other" working capital lines, the tax rate:** still open; the notes read do not break them down.

## Limits of the analysis

- Annual accounts only: no monthly balances, so the working capital peg is indicative.
- Read from the English translations of the reports; the Portuguese originals prevail. The tax and pension notes were not read.
- The 7 key documents of a data room (debt schedule, facility agreements, AR ageing…) are not public (R18).
- A first pass for an analyst to review: no view on the offers, the price or the privatisation.
