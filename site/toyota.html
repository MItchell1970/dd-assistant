Toyota Caetano Portugal

Other income is 60.5% of FY25 EBITDA; note 22 shows €48,078k of stock financing outside reported debt.

Key insights

Three points for the first call

Each with the figure, why it matters in a deal and the question for management.

Most of EBITDA is "other income", and the notes say what it is

Other income was €40,036k in FY25, 60.5% of EBITDA of €66,187k (R04, High). Note 33 breaks it down: recoveries of warranty and other operating costs of €13,977k, operating grants of €5,142k and gains on disposals of assets of €4,862k.

A buyer pays a multiple of EBITDA. Cost recoveries recur while the agreement lasts; a gain on a disposal does not. The split decides the base of the price.

“Which lines of other income are contractual and recurring, and which depend on yearly targets or decisions of the manufacturer?”

There is stock financing outside reported debt

Note 22 says €48,078k is owed to Toyota Kreditbank for vehicle purchases in 2025, inside "other payables". Net financial debt is €76,532k without it.

Financing that funds stock is debt-like for a buyer. If the SPA definition leaves it out, the buyer pays for stock financed by someone else.

“Is vehicle stock financed through floorplan or OEM credit lines, and are these lines included in reported debt?”

EBITDA turned into cash in one year out of three

Operating cash flow was -€12,859k in FY23, €62,857k in FY24 and -€3,987k in FY25 (R10, High). Inventories rose 25.8% in FY25 while COGS rose 4.5% (R09).

The buyer values cash, not EBITDA. A stock build at year-end inflates the working capital peg if it is set on year-end balances.

“What is the composition and ageing of inventory? Which orders support current stock levels?”
