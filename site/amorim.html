Key insights

Three points for the first call

Each with the figure, why it matters in a deal and the question for management.

Strong cash in a weaker year: where did it come from?

Revenue fell 8.3% to €860,967k in FY25, but operating cash flow was €219,015k, 155.3% of EBITDA. Inventories fell from €466,545k to €388,297k. In FY23 the same ratio was 14.9% (R10).

Releasing working capital produces cash once. A buyer normalises it out of free cash flow and checks that the peg is not set after the release.

“Which working capital items explain operating cash above EBITDA in FY25?”

Working capital is half of revenue

Net working capital is €443,570k in FY25, 51.5% of revenue, and €71,514k below the average of the three year-ends.

With this much working capital, the peg is one of the largest price items. It needs 12 to 24 monthly balances, because cork is bought seasonally.

“What are the monthly working capital balances for the last 24 months?”

The notes change what counts as debt

Debt due within 12 months fell from 56.3% in FY24 to 28.7% in FY25 (R11). Note 22 shows the 2024 figure was commercial paper of €55,150k and a bond of €30,000k falling due. Note 24 shows that "other financial liabilities" of €43,264k are mostly sundry creditors, taxes and VAT.

A line called "financial" is not always debt. Reading the note moves most of it from net debt to working capital, which changes both the price deduction and the peg.
